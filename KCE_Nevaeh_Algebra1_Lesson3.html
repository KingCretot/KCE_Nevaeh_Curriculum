Two equations, one question

Lessons 1 and 2 covered finding slope and writing a full linear equation. This lesson puts two of those equations in the same problem and asks a new question: where do they agree? That's a system of equations.

two lines, one crossing point

What a system is

System of equations

Two or more equations, considered together, where you're hunting for the one (x, y) pair that makes all of them true at once.

the one point both agree on

One equation on its own has endless (x, y) pairs that work. A second equation narrows that down — hard — to a single point, or sometimes none at all.

Why it matters

App A: $3 pickup fee, then $2 per mile.   App B: no pickup fee, $2.50 per mile.

At exactly what distance do they cost the same?

cost vs. distance, both apps

Short trip, B wins. Long trip, A wins. That crossover question — where two things become equal — is what a system solves.

For an exact answer: substitution

Substitution method

Solve one equation for a variable, then substitute that expression into the other equation. Use it when the exact value matters and at least one equation is already solved for y.

set the two y's equal

Here's the logic: at the crossing point, BOTH equations are describing the same y — same x, same y, at the same time. So whatever the first equation says y equals, the second equation has to say the exact same thing. That means you can set the two right-hand sides equal to each other and solve for x. Graphing shows you the shape; substitution gets you the exact number, every time.

Solved, step by step

both lines actually cross right here
System: y = 2x + 1  and  y = −x + 7
Set equal
2x + 1 = −x + 7
Add x
3x + 1 = 7
Subtract 1
3x = 6
Divide by 3
x = 2
Plug back in
y = 2(2) + 1 = 5
Solution: (2, 5). Setting the two equations equal worked because both were describing the same y at that point — their right sides HAD to match. Check it against the other original equation to be sure: −(2) + 7 = 5. It matches.

Grandma's seed suppliers

Supplier A: $10 delivery fee + $3 per packet.   Supplier B: no fee, $4 per packet.

At how many packets do they cost the same?

cost vs. packets — where they match
A: y = 3x + 10    B: y = 4x
Set equal
3x + 10 = 4x
Subtract 3x
10 = x
Solve for y
y = 4(10) = 40
At 10 packets, both cost $40. Below 10, Supplier A wins; above 10, Supplier B wins.

Every word from today

What does the solution to a system of equations represent?

A solution must satisfy every equation in the system simultaneously, not just one.

2 of 5

3 of 5
Solve: y = 4x + 2 and y = x + 11. What is x?

4x + 2 = x + 11 → 3x + 2 = 11 → 3x = 9 → x = 3.

Identical slope and intercept means it's the same line twice — every point on it is a solution.
