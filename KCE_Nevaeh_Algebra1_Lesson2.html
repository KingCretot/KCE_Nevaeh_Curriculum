Writing Linear Equations

Slope-Intercept → y = mx + b (you have slope AND the y-intercept)
Point-Slope → y − y₁ = m(x − x₁) (slope + one point, not the y-intercept)

From slope to a full equation

Lesson 1 covered finding the slope of a line. This lesson uses that same slope to write the full equation — meaning you can predict any point on the line without ever needing to see the graph. The bar above stays pinned the whole lesson.

slope + a starting point = the whole line

Slope-intercept form

y = mx + b

m is the slope. b is the y-intercept — the exact point where the line crosses the y-axis, where x = 0. Once you know both, you can write the whole equation in seconds.

crosses at 7, climbs steeply (m = 4)
A line has slope 4 and crosses the y-axis at 7.
m
4
b
7
y = 4x + 7. Done — the hard part is usually that you're NOT handed both pieces this cleanly.

When you only have one point

James's girlfriend Lucy does freelance graphic design on weekends. She charged a client $130 for 4 hours of work, and her rate is $20 per hour — but you don't know her flat setup fee.

How do you write an equation for her total charge?

y − y₁ = m(x − x₁)

(x₁, y₁) is whatever point you were given. Use this when you have the slope and ONE point, but not the y-intercept — then rearrange into y = mx + b.

one known point + a slope

Solving Lucy's rate problem

total charge, starting fee plus hourly rate
m = 20, point = (4, 130)
Plug in
y − 130 = 20(x − 4)
Distribute
y − 130 = 20x − 80
Add 130
y = 20x + 50
y = 20x + 50. That "50" is her flat setup fee — information you never would've known just from the hourly rate.

Combining skills: two points, no slope given

only two points to start — slope isn't given
Points: (1, 5) and (3, 13)
Find slope first (Lesson 1)
m = (13−5)÷(3−1) = 4
Plug into point-slope
y − 5 = 4(x − 1)
Simplify
y − 5 = 4x − 4 → y = 4x + 1
This exact combination — find the slope yourself, then write the equation — is what test questions love. Two formulas working together.

Reading a graph straight into an equation

Crosses the y-axis at 2, rises 1 for every 2 it runs right.
Read b off the graph
b = 2
Read slope (rise/run)
m = 1/2
y = (1/2)x + 2

Every word from today

Slope-intercept form
y = mx + b — the standard, most useful way to write a linear equation.
y-intercept (b)
Where the line crosses the y-axis, where x = 0.
Point-slope form
y − y₁ = m(x − x₁) — used when you have slope + one point, not the y-intercept.
Linear equation
Any equation whose graph is a straight line.

Quiz — show what you know

1 of 5
A line has slope 5 and y-intercept 3. What's its equation?

y = mx + b. m = 5, b = 3, so y = 5x + 3.

2 of 5
You know a slope and ONE point, but not the y-intercept. Which formula first?

Point-slope form is built exactly for this — slope plus one point, no y-intercept given.

3 of 5
A line has slope 2 and passes through (3, 10). What's the y-intercept?

y − 10 = 2(x − 3) → y − 10 = 2x − 6 → y = 2x + 4. b = 4.

4 of 5
You're given two points and no slope. What's the correct first step?

Slope comes first from the two points, then plug it into point-slope form.

5 of 5
On a graph, the line crosses the y-axis at 6 and rises 3 for every 1 it runs right. Equation?

b = 6, m = 3 (rise 3, run 1). y = mx + b gives y = 3x + 6.
