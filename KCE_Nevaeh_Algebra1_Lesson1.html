Rate of Change & Slope

Rate of change → change in output ÷ change in input (word problems, money, distance)
Slope → m = rise ÷ run = (y₂−y₁)÷(x₂−x₁) (graphs or two points)

Things that change

Every day you're surrounded by things that go up, go down, or stay flat — your phone battery, your paycheck, the temperature outside. This lesson puts a name and a number on exactly how fast those things change: rate of change, and its graph version, slope. The bar above stays pinned the whole lesson — glance up any time.

What "rate of change" means

Rate of change

How much one thing changes for every one unit that something else changes — usually written as a ratio: change in the thing you're tracking, divided by change in time.

the ratio, pictured

You already think this way constantly. "My phone loses 5% battery an hour." "I make $12 an hour." "The team scored 3 points a minute." Math just wants it precise, with a formula behind it.

Worked example — the drive to grandma's

James is driving you and Lucy out to grandma's, a 180-mile trip. Once you hit the highway the GPS says 150 miles to go. Forty-five minutes later, it says 105 miles to go.

What's the rate of change of the distance remaining?

miles left to go, ticking down
Change in distance
105 − 150 = −45 miles
Change in time
45 min = 0.75 hr
Rate of change
−45 ÷ 0.75 = −60 mph
That negative sign isn't a mistake — it means the distance is going DOWN. A negative rate of change always means something is decreasing.

Same idea, new name: slope

Slope

The rate of change of a straight line, specifically once you can see it on a graph. Same math as before — slope is just what rate of change is called once there's a line in front of you. Symbol: m.

steepness = rise / run

Plot the road trip on a graph — hours across the bottom, miles-remaining up the side — and you'd get a line heading downward. Its steepness IS the −60 you just calculated.

Four types of slope

Positive

Up left-to-right. A paycheck growing with more hours.

Negative

Down left-to-right. A phone battery on a long drive.

Zero

Flat. Cruise control holding a steady 60 mph.

Undefined

Vertical. A flagpole — no sideways distance to divide by.

Finding slope from a graph

Two points on the line: (1, 2) and (5, 6)
Run (x-change)
5 − 1 = 4
Rise (y-change)
6 − 2 = 4
Slope
m = 4 ÷ 4 = 1
Shortcut for next time: m = (y₂ − y₁) ÷ (x₂ − x₁) — rise over run, written with coordinates instead of a picture.

One more — no family in this one

A part-time job: after 3 hours you've earned $36. After 7 hours, $84.

earnings, hour by hour
Change in earnings
$84 − $36 = $48
Change in hours
7 − 3 = 4
Rate of change
$48 ÷ 4 = $12/hr
That's the hourly wage — positive slope, because total earnings only ever go up the longer you work. Same math as the road trip, opposite direction.

Every word from today

Rate of change
How fast something changes compared to something else, usually time.
Slope
Rate of change, specifically on a graph. Symbol: m.
Rise / Run
Vertical change / horizontal change between two points.
Positive / Negative slope
Line rises / falls left to right — something increasing / decreasing.
Zero / Undefined slope
Flat line, nothing changing / vertical line, can't be calculated.
